# Written Exam: Instructions

The exam has two parts: two short written essays, then a short oral follow-up with the instructor. Together they ask you to plan realistic analytics projects and explain your thinking.

The questions are in [questions.md](questions.md).

## Part 1: Written essays (50% of exam grade)

**What to write**

- Answer **two** questions: Question 1 (required) and **one** of Questions 2–5.
- Write about **500 words** per answer (roughly 450–550).
- Each question asks you to plan a future project in a setting you choose. Name your setting in the first sentence of each answer.
- Use the numbered points under each question as a guide. You don't need headings for each one, but your answer should cover them.

**Format and submission**

- Submit one document with both answers, each labeled with its question number.
- Put the word count at the end of each answer.
- Submit by the posted due date.

**Resources**

- You may use your own notes and all course materials: seminar slides, lab handouts and your lab work.
- **Generative AI tools are allowed.** You may use them to brainstorm, outline, draft or edit. You are fully responsible for everything you submit: in the oral follow-up you must be able to explain each answer and the rationale behind it in your own words. Treat AI output the way this course teaches, checking it before you rely on it.
- If you draw on an outside source, cite it briefly.

**How essays are graded**

Essays are graded on **participation**. You earn full credit for submitting two complete, on-topic answers of about 500 words each, on time. The essays are not graded for polish; they are the starting point for the oral follow-up.

## Part 2: Oral follow-up (50% of exam grade)

**What to expect**

- After submission, each student meets with the instructor for **5–10 minutes** by video (or in person).
- The instructor will ask follow-up questions about your essays: why you made a choice, what you would do if something changed, how you would handle a specific problem, or how you would use and check AI in the project you planned.
- Have your submitted essays open. You may refer to them but not to other materials.
- Schedule your session using the posted sign-up. Contact the instructor in advance if you need an accommodation.

**Example follow-up questions**

- "Your stakeholders disagree on the definition you chose. How would you resolve it?"
- "Your first results look much better than expected. What would you check before sharing them?"
- "How would you know whether your program worked, and not just that enrolled patients were different?"
- "Where would an AI tool help in this project, and how would you verify its output?"

## Oral rubric

The oral follow-up is scored on five criteria, each aligned with the course learning outcomes. Each criterion is scored 1–4, for a total of 20 points, which is scaled to 50% of the exam grade.

| Criterion | Learning outcome | 4: Exemplary | 3: Proficient | 2: Developing | 1: Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Project design** | Design and run an analytics project, including intermediate steps and stakeholder deliverables | Plan is complete and realistic; adapts it convincingly when conditions change | Plan covers the main steps; adapts with some prompting | Plan has gaps (missing stakeholders, requirements or deliverable); struggles to adapt | Cannot describe a coherent plan |
| **Domain application** | Explain how analytics improves performance in revenue cycle, value-based payment and population health | Connects the project to domain concepts, policy and incentives accurately and specifically | Uses domain concepts correctly with minor gaps | Uses domain terms loosely or with some errors | Little or inaccurate use of domain concepts |
| **Data and methods** | Access and query data in relational databases; use machine learning to find insights | Chooses appropriate data, grain and methods and explains why; anticipates data problems | Chooses reasonable data and methods with sound reasoning | Data or method choices are vague or partly mismatched to the question | Cannot explain what data or methods the project needs |
| **Quality and responsible AI** | Explain best practices for effective and ethical use of AI; evaluate AI-generated outputs with quality assurance approaches | Describes specific checks, including how to verify AI output; identifies ethical and equity risks and how to address them | Names relevant checks and at least one risk | Mentions quality or AI concerns only generally | Does not address quality, AI or ethics |
| **Reasoning and communication** | Design stakeholder deliverables; communicate findings | Answers clearly and directly, states uncertainty, and tailors the explanation to a decision maker | Answers clearly with minor gaps | Answers are hard to follow or avoid the question | Cannot explain or defend the essay's reasoning |
