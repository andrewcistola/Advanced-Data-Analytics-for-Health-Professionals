# Written Exam: Questions

Answer **two** questions in total:

- **Question 1** (required)
- **One** of Questions 2–5 (your choice)

Each answer should be about **500 words**. Each question asks you to plan a future analytics project in a setting you choose: a workplace you know, one you expect to work in, or a realistic one you describe. State your setting in the first sentence of each answer. You may use the same setting for both answers or a different one for each.

See [instructions.md](instructions.md) for format, submission and grading.

---

## Question 1 (required): Plan an analytics project from question to deliverable

Choose a decision in your setting that better data could improve. Plan the analytics project that would support it.

Address:

1. **The decision.** State the business question as a decision. Who makes it, and what will they do differently depending on the answer?
2. **Stakeholders.** Who are the sponsor, decision maker, end users, data owners and subject-matter experts? How will you align them on goal, scope and definitions?
3. **Requirements.** Define the population, time period and key terms. Write at least one acceptance criterion that would tell you the work is done and correct.
4. **Data and quality assurance.** What data sources will you use, and at what grain? Name two quality checks you will run and when.
5. **Deliverable and timeline.** What format will the deliverable take, and why does it fit the decision? Name the step most likely to delay the project and how you will manage expectations around it.

*Draws on:* Components of a Data Analytics Project; Stakeholder Alignment Strategies; Writing Specifications and Requirements; Overview of Quality Assurance Strategies; Designing the Right Stakeholder Deliverables; Timeline Planning and Expectation Management.

## Question 2: Revenue cycle improvement

Plan a project to reduce lost or delayed revenue in your setting, such as claim denials, slow payment or manual prior authorization.

Address:

1. Which problem you will target and why it matters financially.
2. The measures you will use (for example, initial denial rate, days to payment, recovery after denial or electronic prior authorization share), defined precisely enough to calculate.
3. The data you need (claims, remittances, reason codes, authorization records) and how you will turn raw codes into categories someone can act on.
4. One current policy or industry change that affects the project, and how.
5. How you will know the project succeeded, and one common pitfall you will avoid.

*Draws on:* Revenue Cycle Basics; Claims Data Analytics; Transaction Standards and Electronic Adoption; Writing Specifications and Requirements.

## Question 3: Value-based payment performance

Plan a project to measure and improve performance under a value-based arrangement in your setting, such as an ACO, a Medicare Advantage plan, a practice in a shared-savings contract or a hospital quality program.

Address:

1. How patients will be attributed to providers, and what could make that attribution wrong.
2. One quality or cost measure, specified with its population, numerator, exclusions and measurement period.
3. How you will handle small denominators and differences in how sick patients are, so comparisons are fair.
4. How risk adjustment or documentation affects your results, and how you will stay on the right side of compliance.
5. How you will present results to clinicians or leaders so they trust and act on them.

*Draws on:* Value Based Payment Current State; Quality Measure Calculation; Why VBP Data Management is So Difficult; Risk Adjustment; Communicating Complex Findings.

## Question 4: Population health program design and evaluation

Plan a project to design and evaluate an intervention for a population in your setting, such as transitional care after discharge, complex case management or placing a community resource.

Address:

1. The population and the outcome you want to change.
2. How you will choose who receives the intervention, including any area-level or social data, and how you will focus on people the program can actually help.
3. How you will evaluate whether the program worked, including how you will avoid selection bias.
4. One equity or ethical consideration in targeting or evaluation.
5. What result would lead you to recommend expanding the program, changing it or stopping it.

*Draws on:* Population Health Overview; Evidence on Complex Case Management Interventions; Area Level Resources and Spatial Analytics; Handling Obstacles, Setbacks, and Errors.

## Question 5: Choosing where to invest

Your organization can fund one analytics-driven program next year. Plan a project to recommend which one. Compare two or three realistic options from different domains, such as denial prevention, closing documentation gaps, transitional care, targeted community outreach or a payment integrity review.

Address:

1. The options, and the decision maker and budget constraint.
2. How you will estimate each option's return, including costs, events avoided and their value, and the main assumptions.
3. How you will handle uncertainty, for example with ranges or sensitivity to key assumptions.
4. Which stakeholders' goals compete, and how you will reach a decision they accept.
5. How you will communicate the recommendation, and what you will do if early results disappoint.

*Draws on:* Cross Domain Applications; Fraud, Waste, and Abuse; Stakeholder Alignment Strategies; Communicating Complex Findings; Handling Obstacles, Setbacks, and Errors.
